I passed the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam on September 30. I got there by taking practice exams until my scores moved from the 400s to the 800s and 900s. The passing score is 700.

That method will not carry over to the next exam.

Here is what I did, and what I plan to do.

## Why Cloud Practitioner first

Cloud Practitioner is the entry-level AWS exam. I graduate in December and I am about to start applying for jobs, and I wanted a certification on my resume before the first application went out. That was most of the reason. It was a formality, and a quick one.

I did think about skipping it and going straight to the Associate exam. I decided against it. The broad base of general AWS knowledge would help me long term, whichever exam came next.

So it is a resume line now, and a foundation for the Solutions Architect Associate exam in December.

## The loop

A study block was about two hours. Every block had the same three pieces: a full practice exam, an AWS course module, and a second full practice exam. I alternated between the AWS practice exams and CloudCertPro from block to block.

The first blocks ran long. By the end I was finishing a full exam in 20 to 30 minutes, and the modules were quick too, so two exams and a module fit inside the two hours. I studied five days a week and fit the blocks around school. All of my classes are async, which makes that easy.

It was not a complicated system, but it was a lot of questions.

## Why the loop will not carry over

Cloud Practitioner is 65 questions in 90 minutes, and for most people it is like a vocabulary test. The concepts are foundational, so the loop was built to drill terms until I stopped mixing them up.

The Solutions Architect Associate exam is also 65 questions, but it gives you 130 minutes and the questions are scenarios about trade-offs, not definitions. The exam guide splits it into four domains: secure architectures at 30 percent, resilient architectures at 26, high-performing architectures at 24, and cost-optimized architectures at 20. Finishing in 20 to 30 minutes is not my goal here.

The new plan is built around the domains.

## The plan for December 15

The plan runs from October 1 to December 15. That gives me enough time to focus on finishing school and my undergrad research project while still completing the certification before my graduation ceremony. Every week has the same structure. On Monday and Wednesday I answer 40 cold questions and log every miss. On Tuesday and Thursday I review the misses, and some weeks I also build a lab. On Friday I redo the miss list.

The weeks follow the domains in order of weight. Secure architectures get two weeks, then resilient, then high-performing. Cost-optimized architectures get one. Each topic week ends with a quiz that has a score gate, from 65 percent in the first week up to 70 percent. A lab is not done until I have deleted what I built.

Full timed practice exams are no longer the daily unit. They are four checkpoints, each with a higher gate than the last:

- Exam A, the weekend of October 31: 60 percent
- Exam B, November 22: 68 percent
- Exam C, December 1: 75 percent
- Exam D, December 13: 78 percent

Thanksgiving week is half the hours and the miss list only. The last full week is my weakest domains and the miss list, 90 minutes a day. It collides with finals week and my birthday, so it will be a busy week to send it off, but I find that exciting and fitting. On December 14 I check my ID and my booking.

## What is happening outside of certs

I am finishing my final semester at FIU and undertaking an independent undergrad research project combining my passion for IT and Communication studies. More will be on the site when I am able to post about it. I will graduate cum laude and I am proud of that achievement. Aside from that, I have a plan to refresh my skills with Python and Bash so I can really crush some technical interviews.

I am proud of my progress, both at university and with my personal portfolio. But, like most people in this field, I have countless topics I still want to get hands-on experience with.
